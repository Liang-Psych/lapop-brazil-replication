# Original Thesis Information / 原论文信息

## The Impact of Chinese Development Finance on Brazilian Public Attitudes Toward China

## Disclaimer

This repository contains replication code and data-processing scripts for the master’s thesis identified below.

The materials currently contained in this repository correspond to the original thesis analysis. An issue concerning the coding direction and interpretation of the dependent variable was identified on July 26, 2026. The original empirical findings should therefore be treated as uncorrected and should not be cited as validated results while the correction process is underway.

## Original Abstract — Uncorrected

> **Status:** The following abstract is retained for documentation of the original thesis version. Its empirical conclusions are under correction.

This study aims to explore whether and how Chinese development finance activities in Brazil influence local public attitudes toward China in the context of China’s growing global economic influence. By integrating survey data from the **AmericasBarometer (2012–2023)** with geocoded project data from **AidData’s Global Chinese Development Finance Geospatial Dataset**, this research constructs a comprehensive dataset of **4,318 Brazilian respondents across 499 cities**. It also combines this dataset with case studies of the **Belo Monte Ultra-High-Voltage Transmission Line project** and the **China-Brazil Science and Technology Courtyard project**, adopting a mixed-methods approach that systematically analyzes the relationship between Chinese development finance and Brazilian public attitudes toward China.

The original thesis reported a significant positive correlation between Chinese development finance projects and local Brazilian attitudes toward China and interpreted this relationship as evidence that Chinese development finance could generate positive soft-power returns. It also reported heterogeneous effects associated with local development levels and individual news-media exposure.

A subsequent review identified an issue involving the coding direction and interpretation of the dependent variable. Preliminary corrected analyses indicate that the originally reported positive relationship is not robust after the dependent variable is correctly interpreted and relevant temporal and inferential adjustments are introduced. Accordingly, the original empirical conclusions and heterogeneity findings should not be treated as validated results.

A formal erratum, corrected analyses, and revised replication files are currently being prepared.

**Keywords:** International Development Cooperation; Chinese Development Finance; China’s Foreign Aid; Soft Power; Public Attitudes
